  audio    three_utterances.wav

NOTE: the default audio is three synthetic tone bursts, NOT speech. It proves
the pipeline moves and says nothing about how well a model hears Thai. Point
--audio at a real recording for that.

==============================================================================
STAGE 1  normalise - the only place that knows a phone is not 16 kHz mono
==============================================================================
Everything above this stage is promised 16 kHz mono float32, always. A phone
line delivers none of that, so it is converted once, here, at the edge.

  what arrived                        bytes  ->    samples    rate
  --------------------------------------------------------------
  8 kHz mono mu-law (US, Japan)         160  ->        320   16000
  8 kHz mono A-law (THAILAND)           160  ->        320   16000
  8 kHz STEREO mu-law                   320  ->        320   16000
  16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM                640  ->        320   16000

  Every row is 20 ms of audio and every row comes out as 320 samples at 16 kHz.
  Nothing above this line ever has to ask which one it got.

  Why BOTH companding laws exist here, shown rather than argued:
    A-law idle bytes read correctly as A-law  -> level 0.0002  (silence)
    the same bytes read as mu-law by mistake  -> level 0.0219  (LOUD)
    It does not crash. It produces loud plausible noise, and the first thing
    anybody blames is the microphone or the model. Thailand is A-law.

==============================================================================
STAGE 2 + 3  detect voice, then decide where the sentence starts and ends
==============================================================================
The detector answers one narrow question per 32 ms frame: is anyone speaking?
It returns a PROBABILITY, not a verdict. Deciding where an utterance begins and
ends is a separate job with its own rules, and those rules are inherited from the
team's earlier Thai project rather than invented here:

    speech threshold      0.65     (Silero's own default is 0.5)
    minimum speech        500 ms   below this it is a cough
    minimum silence       100 ms   Thai pauses mid-clause
    pad BEFORE the start  120 ms   <- the important one
    pad AFTER the end     60 ms

  The leading pad is the one worth understanding. Whisper clips the first
  syllable without it, and in Thai the first syllable often carries the tone that
  decides which word it is. So the segment starts 120 ms BEFORE the detector
  noticed anybody - 1920 samples of audio it would otherwise have thrown away.

  Reading three_utterances.wav (4.5s, 16000 Hz)

      time    level   speech?   what the detector saw
  --------------------------------------------------------------
     0.02s   0.0006      0.00   <-- back to silence
     0.32s   0.1212      1.00   --> SPEECH
     1.22s   0.0006      0.00   <-- back to silence
     1.72s   0.1359      1.00   --> SPEECH
     2.82s   0.0006      0.00   <-- back to silence
     3.42s   0.1387      1.00   --> SPEECH
     4.12s   0.0006      0.00   <-- back to silence

  Those transitions are what the endpointer turns into whole sentences.

==============================================================================
THE THREE GUARDS  (why they exist: a real GPU run, not a theory)
==============================================================================
Fed one second of DIGITAL SILENCE, Whisper on this laptop took 8578 ms and
returned invented Thai. Fed real speech energy it took 155 ms. So a detector
false positive is not a junk line on a screen, it is a 55x delay that blocks
every real sentence queued behind it.

Worse: fed our own vocabulary hint, it handed those very words back as if the
caller had said them - and those are exactly the words that make a brief look
credible. Three guards catch it. Here they are, judging real strings:

                                       loop?   echo?   verdict
  --------------------------------------------------------------------
  what the GPU returned on silence     False    True   REFUSED
  a repetition loop                     True   False   REFUSED
  a genuine caller sentence            False   False   reaches the agent

  (15 vocabulary terms, read from config/stt_vocabulary.yaml)
  The third row is the one that matters most: a guard that ate real sentences
  containing insurance words would delete exactly the calls it exists to help.
