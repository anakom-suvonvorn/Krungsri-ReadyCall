The finding

We built a service desk. The brief is asking for a sales desk.

The submitted deck answers one question: a customer is already calling — how do we make that call better? Context before the call, routing, a brief on the screen. That is real, it is built, and it maps to a named in-scope bullet: “Broker productivity tools (เช่น สรุปลูกค้าให้ broker ก่อนคุย)”.

Reframe

Broker, not agent — and you are right that it is mostly configuration

The distinction you drew at the orientation is the load-bearing one. An agent (ตัวแทน) represents one insurer: one-to-one. A broker represents the customer across many insurers: many-to-one. That is not a labelling difference — it is why comparison is the broker's core job at all, and why the biggest leak is where it is.

It also redraws the call taxonomy exactly as you described. A broker owns renewal, enquiry, quoting, selling, policy admin and the relationship. Deep plan adjudication and the claim itself belong to the insurer, and the broker's job on those calls is to hand over well — which is a different outcome from resolving.

You said this is mostly the YAML rather than the logic. That is right, and it is worth saying out loud in the pitch, because it is the strongest feasibility evidence we have: the domain lives in config/ by design, and nothing in services/ is allowed to hardcode an insurance literal. Retargeting is a config swap.

Three things are not config, and they are small but real:

The two additions

Your ideas, polished

Compare & best-fit

Step 3 · the biggest leak

Today a broker compares plans with brochures and PDFs — the brief says so in those words. The result is ตัวเลือกเยอะ ตัดสินใจช้า: too many options, slow decisions, and a recommendation that misses the point.

Build a comparison surface that both sides can see: the customer's current cover in the left column and candidate plans beside it, differences highlighted rather than listed. Then a ranked shortlist with a written reason per row — “covers the ฿3,000/day room rate your current plan caps at ฿1,500”, not a score out of ten.

Two guardrails, and they are what keep it inside the brief's scope rather than outside it:

  • Rank on facts, explain with the model. The ordering comes from real plan attributes against the customer's real gaps. The model writes the sentence; it never invents the number. A figure that cannot be traced to a data field does not render.
  • Surface and compare — never quote or underwrite. Premium pricing and underwriting are explicitly out of scope. Show published plan features and the gap analysis; the broker decides and the insurer quotes.

This is also the answer to “what is the AI actually for”. Summarising a call is useful; closing the mismatch the brief opens with is the pitch.

The tool rail

Step 4 · the drop-off

Your form-filling observation is the best piece of user insight in the notes, and the brief independently confirms the leak: เอกสารเยอะ ลูกค้า drop-off กลางทาง — too many documents, customers walk away mid-process.

The idea: stop describing a website down the phone. Bind the call to the customer's screen, and let the broker put the right thing on it.

Two entry paths, converging:

  • From a normal phone call — the broker presses send link, an SMS/LINE message goes to the number already on the call, the customer opens it and signs in, then taps “ให้เจ้าหน้าที่ช่วย”. Call and screen are now paired.
  • From inside the app — already signed in, so the pairing is implicit from the first second. This is the path the existing Contact flow already builds.

Then the broker works a rail of tools: push a form and watch it fill, request a document, capture digits, start a signature, run OCR on a photographed ID. The customer never navigates anywhere. Things simply arrive.

Two things make this more credible than it sounds for a week of work:

  • The pattern already exists. Keypad capture is exactly this shape — a control the broker triggers that changes what the customer's device is doing, with the result landing on the workstation. The rail generalises a thing that is built, rather than inventing one.
  • Honest stubs are fine. E-signature and OCR should look and feel real and be clearly stubbed underneath. Nobody will ask you to sign a legally binding document on stage; they will ask whether the flow holds.

One reframe worth making. You described the extreme user — an older customer whose children installed the app. That is a sharp story, but do not build it as a persona: document drop-off is universal, and the brief prices it as a leak for everyone. Use the tech-shy customer as the moment that proves the design, not as the segment it serves.

Correction

On consent, you are half right — and the half that is wrong is graded

Your reasoning: data arriving from banks, insurers and affiliates was already consented at the point it was collected, so we only need consent for what we gather ourselves — the recording and what comes out of it.

For holding and showing that data to a broker who has been routed the call, that is essentially right, and it matches how the system already works: what a broker may see is a different question from what they may say and do.

But the briefing gates something else, in writing:

PDPA — ต้องมี consent ชัดเจนก่อนใช้ข้อมูลวิเคราะห์/แนะนำเฉพาะบุคคล

ข้อมูลสุขภาพเป็น sensitive data ต้องขอ consent แยก

Briefing, section 03 — Privacy / Regulation constraints

Read those against what we are now building. Personalised analysis and recommendation is exactly the compare-and-best-fit feature — the brief requires explicit consent before it runs, regardless of where the underlying data came from. So the consent gate does not disappear; it moves, from “may we hold this” to “may we use this to recommend”.

And the second line settles a question that has been open and blocking: health data needs separate consent, stated by the brief itself. That is no longer a judgement call about how strictly a regulator reads PDPA — it is a judging criterion. The recommendation stands and is now backed by the document: name the health scope in the offer wording on health lines, and gate extraction in code so health details can never be pulled without it.

Handled well this is a scoring opportunity, not a tax. Under Impact and User insight, a consent moment that is one clear keypress and visibly changes what the system does is far stronger than a privacy policy nobody reads — and it directly answers the brief's fourth constraint about decisioning that must not become discriminatory.

Your question

One command on any machine — yes, and mostly already there

The compose file already runs Postgres, Redis, MinIO and a database browser. What is missing is the application itself. Three pieces:

  • A multi-stage Dockerfile: Node builds the workstation and the customer app, then a Python image copies the built assets and runs the API.
  • An app service in the compose file, wired to the containers already there.
  • A demo profile so docker compose --profile demo up brings up everything in one command.

The honest caveat: the GPU does not come with it

The real Thai speech engine needs the machine's GPU, and a plain container will not reach it without extra host setup that varies by machine — exactly the kind of thing that fails at a venue.

So ship the container on the stage-safe scripted engine, which needs no GPU, no model download and no network, and keep the real engine as a documented host-run option for the machine that has the graphics card. “Runs anywhere; runs the real model on our box” is a better story than a container that only works on one laptop anyway.

The softphone stays stubbed, exactly as you said. It makes the system work; it does not make the flow legible, and it is the single highest-risk dependency in the project. The demo endpoint that places calls has stood in for it since the beginning and will hold for one more week.

Every Thai line in the system has never been spoken aloud — the pack is text rendered by a null engine. Worth fixing before anyone hears the IVR live; not worth a day this week, since the pitch shows screens rather than plays audio.

The rest of the tool rail

Signature, OCR, document upload. Get one tool working properly and stub the others convincingly. A rail with one real tool and four honest placeholders demos better than five that half-work.
